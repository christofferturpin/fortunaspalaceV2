U.S. INTERSTATE ROUTE TAKEN — IN BLUE BALLPOINT, BY ANOTHER HAND, BEFORE YOU WERE BORN

This atlas is older than you. The blue ballpoint marks on page 47 were not put there by you.

Your father grew up wanting to go to Branson. It was a place he talked about. You were too young to understand why. Country music, lakes, a place where people went on vacation. He never went. He never took your mother. He certainly never took you.

The X over Branson is in his handwriting. He drew it sometime in the early nineties, before he met your mother. He kept the atlas. After he was arrested, Sammy boxed his things and you took the box when you turned eighteen.

You drove the route in his car, which became your truck. You stopped at the diner outside Cookeville he had circled. You bought Rebecca at the Goodwill there. You did not know why you bought it. The book has been in the passenger seat since.

When the splash page said a place at the edge of town, when it said the neon is on, even in daylight — those were words you had heard before, in a voice you had been trying for twenty years not to remember.

Branson was never the destination. Branson was the place he was going to take you. You went without him. You went for him. You do not know which.
