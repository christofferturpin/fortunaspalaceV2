EST. 2004 HISTORICAL · DAYTON · TN

The hammer was your father's. He framed houses with it before he framed anything else. He used it on her in 2004 — on a Wednesday, in the afternoon, in the kitchen.

47

That was the number the medical examiner counted. Forty-seven impact sites, most of them to the head and upper body.

He called the Rhea County Sheriff's Office from the front porch when he was done. He sat with the hammer beside him and waited. He did not resist arrest.

At trial he refused the insanity defense. He said he had known what he was doing. He said he would do it again. They pled it down to second because the prosecution did not want to risk a jury who knew about the bathtub.

Fifteen years at Big Sandy. Pancreatic cancer six months after release. He was dead eleven weeks after the diagnosis. You saw him three times in fifteen years and you were not good at it.

Forty-seven blows. The hammer in the lucite is not the hammer. The hammer is not in a museum. The hammer is in evidence storage in Rhea County. The shop sells a reproduction so that the people who remember can have something to hold.
