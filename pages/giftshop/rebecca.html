REBECCA
Daphne du Maurier · Chapter One

Last night I dreamt I went to Manderley again. It seemed to me I stood by the iron gate leading to the drive, and for a while I could not enter, for the way was barred to me.

There was a padlock and a chain upon the gate. I called in my dream to the lodge-keeper, and had no answer, and peering closer through the rusted spokes of the gate I saw that the lodge was uninhabited.

No smoke came from the chimney, and the little lattice windows gaped forlorn. Then, like all dreamers, I was possessed of a sudden with supernatural powers and passed like a spirit through the barrier before me.

Charlotte Perkins Gilman

The front pattern does move — and no wonder! The woman behind shakes it!

Sometimes I think there are a great many women behind, and sometimes only one, and she crawls around fast, and her crawling shakes it all over.

Then in the very bright spots she keeps still, and in the very shady spots she just takes hold of the bars and shakes them hard.

And she is all the time trying to climb through. But nobody could climb through that pattern — it strangles so; I think that is why it has so many heads.

They get through, and then the pattern strangles them off and turns them upside down, and makes their eyes white!

ZANTHIA: DAUGHTER OF THE CROOKED MOON
K. R. Vance · Silverthorn Books, 2004

Zanthia drew the moonsteel blade from its sheath and the runes along the fuller flickered to wakefulness, drinking in the torchlight of the chamber. Across the obsidian dais, Lord Vexilar smiled his terrible smile.

"You cannot kill me, witch-child," he hissed. "I am older than the seven kingdoms. I was old when your grandmother's grandmother was a babe at the breast."

"I don't have to kill you," Zanthia said, and her violet eyes blazed with the inner fire her mother had named the Gift and her father had named the Curse. "I only have to remember you correctly."

She spoke the Word.

Lord Vexilar's smile faltered. Something passed behind his eyes — a flicker, a doubt, a small cold question. Had he been a cruel man? Or had he been a kind one, much wronged? The chamber seemed, for a moment, to hold two versions of him at once, and then three, and then a dozen, each one bleeding faintly into the next like ink in water.

"Stop," he whispered.

"I haven't begun," said Zanthia.

Behind her, the captive princess Aelyndra stirred in her chains and made a sound that was almost, but not quite, a laugh. The torches guttered. Somewhere in the high vaults of the keep, a bell that had not rung in four hundred years began, very softly, to ring.
